BreathBand® app: five design directions

Design exploration for the BreathBand® companion app · November 2026

One product, five ways it could look and feel.

BreathBand® is a soft strap worn around the waist, between the belly button and the bottom of the rib cage. It senses the rise and fall of abdominal breathing and streams it over Bluetooth to an iPhone or iPad app. The app guides breathing sessions, scores how closely you follow a pacer, and syncs every session to BreathCloud, where a coach or clinician can review it remotely.

This page shows five complete design directions for that app. Each one is a different answer to the same question: what should breath training feel like? Some are built for clinicians, some for athletes, some for people who have never trained their breathing before.

How to read this page

Every direction shows the same four screens, with the same sample user and the same session data. Only the design changes: colour, typography, layout, shape, and how the breathing is drawn. Switch to By screen at the top to put one screen from all five directions side by side.

The four screens, and why these four

Home dashboard
What a returning user sees first: their latest score, how they are trending, and one tap to start a session. It sets the everyday tone of the product.
First run: strap tension
A new user can’t do anything until the strap is fitted correctly. The dashboard waits, locked, while a tension gauge guides them to the right fit. This is where first impressions are made.
Live paced session
The heart of the product. The user follows an on-screen pacer, five seconds in and five seconds out, with their own breath drawn on top and musical notes for eyes-closed practice.
Session review
The result: a Total Breath Score, timing and depth broken down for inhale and exhale, and the full breath trace against the pacer. It is the screen users share and coaches study.

What stays the same in every direction

  • Inhale is red, exhale is blue. A fixed colour code, so a breath reads the same way on every screen. Each is also labelled, so the code never depends on colour vision alone.
  • IN at the top, OUT at the bottom. Every waveform rises with the inhale and falls with the exhale around a dashed midline, the way the body moves.
  • Scores from 1 to 10, always with a word. Staying within 10% of the pacer scores 10, and each further 10% off costs one point. A number never appears alone: “7.5 · Good”.
  • Readable by everyone. Text sized for comfortable reading, contrast that meets WCAG AA, and touch targets of at least 44 px.

Design questions still open

Each direction commits to an answer so the screens look finished. These choices don’t depend on the direction, so whichever one goes forward can take either answer.

  1. Circle or line as the default pacer.A circle that grows and shrinks is easiest to follow at a glance. A scrolling line shows timing errors more precisely. Four directions lead with the circle; Coach Console leads with the line. Both can live in the app, with one as the default.
  2. Numbers on the live session screen.Clinical Precision, Night Performance and Coach Console show elapsed and remaining time. Soft Organic and Playful Progress show progress without digits, through a filling ring or breath beads, so the screen stays calm during eyes-closed practice.
  3. Where the waveform turns from red to blue.Here the trace is red above the midline and blue below it, matching how inhale and exhale depth are measured. The alternative is red while the breath rises and blue while it falls, which follows the breath phase instead.
  4. The words that go with scores.Proposed: 9–10 Excellent, 7–8 Good, 5–6 Fair, 3–4 Building, 1–2 Starting out. They set the product’s voice, anywhere from clinical to encouraging.
